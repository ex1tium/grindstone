# Foundation v4.10 architecture review 1

Scope: pull request #12, branch `docs/foundation-v4-10-vision`, reviewed exactly as of commit `55d04a2` (`git diff main 55d04a2`): ADR-48 to ADR-53, S7, SPEC-28, R5, Q-12, Q-13, the tab changes that follow them, the README and status changes, and the forward notes in SPEC-02, SPEC-04 to SPEC-10. The playbook asks for this review because the change touches the concepts, two invariants (I-14, I-15), the module ownership table and the SDK ports. Checked against ADR-01 to ADR-47, I-01 to I-20, QS-01 to QS-15, the threat and failure models, the module map (`scripts/ff/module_map.py`), the ownership table, the validation checklist and the accepted M1 specifications.

**Verdict: changes required.** The direction is sound and the hard requirement is met in shape: work nests in the intent graph, factories stay flat peers, any mix of factories can serve one goal, and ADR-52 rejects nested orchestrators and nested factories for the right reasons. ADR-48, ADR-50 and ADR-51 are well bounded, and ADR-53 keeps auto-merge out of the MVP. One finding blocks acceptance: ADR-52 lets a charter cover `never`-class and flagged decisions, which contradicts I-07, the flag rule in the Decision concept, I-20 and validation item 23, and the direction record should not settle that question at all (F1). Seven major findings follow. Most concern ADR-49, whose rebase, review carry-over and post-answer subject change are not yet tied to I-05, I-09, I-15 and the decision lifecycle, and the kernel/factory seam the queue and continuation need. The rest are consistency fixes. None of them needs the shape to change.

## Blocking

**F1 (blocking). A charter may cover `never`-class and flagged decisions, against I-07, the flag rule, I-20 and validation item 23.**
Location: ADR-52, "Modes": "In every mode a `never`-class or flagged decision, a `permission` decision and an architectural change stay with a person unless the charter names that exact case; anything `untrusted` always does (I-07)". The same clause appears in Concepts, Decision, "Orchestrator (after the MVP, ADR-52)"; in the SDLC decisions paragraph ("An orchestrator … may answer one of these only where its charter names that exact case"); in "Charters" ("the irreversible actions allowed (each named exactly, under I-20)"); and in the risk row "Text agents wrote steers the orchestrator's authority".
Problem:
- I-07 says scope expansion *and* untrusted content need a person, enforced by both flags being class `never`. ADR-52 carves out only `untrusted`, so a charter naming a `scope_expansion` case breaks I-07. No record amends I-07.
- The unchanged Decision text still says `never` means "always a person" and "A flagged decision always needs a person, at every autonomy level". The concept now contradicts itself within one section.
- I-20 lets an irreversible action skip the person only when "an approved runbook covers [it] exactly". The text never says a charter entry is such a runbook. Validation item 23 says every irreversible action "reaches a person".
- `manual_check` is a decision kind, so a charter could name it and the orchestrator would produce gating evidence. That contradicts its own rule "never makes or proves a subject".
- The risk row lists `untrusted` among the cases that stay with a person "unless named exactly", which contradicts ADR-52's "always".
- This is detail, and ADR-35 and ADR-52's own status say detail waits for the R2 addendum.
Fix: in ADR-52 and every tab that repeats it, state the direction: "`never`-class and flagged decisions, `permission`, `manual_check` and irreversible actions stay with a person; whether a charter may name any exact case of them is decided by its own record with the R2 addendum, which must amend I-07, I-20 and the flag rule if it allows one." Drop "each named exactly, under I-20" from the charter fields, or say that no irreversible action may be chartered until that record. Correct the risk row to match.

## Major

**F2 (major). The relation between charters and autonomy levels, the instance ceiling and the tripwire is unstated, so a charter can bypass ADR-30 and ADR-53.**
Location: ADR-52, "Charters" and "Modes" ("the tripwire and pause act on the orchestrator as on policy"); glossary "Autonomy level … the orchestrator's charter is separate"; ADR-53 "policy, or an orchestrator whose charter grants it, may answer it once …".
Problem: the tripwire drops a project to L1, but no text says what L1 means for a charter. Revision 2's "the tripwire demotes charters to Assist" was cut in revision 3. Nothing caps a charter by the eligibility class or the project's level. As written, a Checkpointed charter on an L1 project could answer an `elevated` (`l3`) `delivery`. That is auto-merge without L3, and it sidesteps ADR-53's earned-trust count and the instance ceiling. ADR-53 binds the orchestrator for the routine class only.
Fix: add one direction sentence to ADR-52, mirrored in the Decision concept: "A charter never lets the orchestrator answer a kind above what the eligibility class allows at the project's current autonomy level and the instance ceiling; a tripwire trip or pause reduces every charter in the project to Assist until the operator restores it." If the operator wants charters to be independent of levels, say that explicitly and record why, because it changes ADR-30's predictability argument.

**F3 (major). A `delivery` answer is not bound to the subject the queue finally merges.**
Location: ADR-49, "Decision": "served one at a time in the order its `delivery` decisions were answered … When the base has moved, the factory's workspace provider updates the subject … After the `delivery` decision Grindstone completes the delivery itself". ADR-53: "a delivery whose class or context changes after a batch was answered leaves the batch and is asked again".
Problem: the person answers `delivery` for subject X. The queue may then merge X′, a new subject (ADR-49 says so). After a conflict resolution X′ carries a changed patch the person never saw. The decision lifecycle refuses an answer only *before* it is given ("its context hash changed"). ADR-53 re-asks only for batched answers, so a single answer has a weaker rule than a batch. This is the same "approval of something the person did not see" that ADR-53 rejects for batches.
Fix: state in ADR-49 and the Delivery port text what a `delivery` answer binds to. For example: "the change (for SDLC, the `sdlc.patch` hash), the target and the evidence set it was shown; when the queue's update leaves the change identical and the re-run mechanical checks pass, the answer stands; any other change to the subject re-raises the decision." Then make ADR-53's re-ask rule the same for single and batched answers.

**F4 (major). Carrying review evidence over a rebase on `sdlc.patch` is an undeclared exception to I-05, and the kernel mechanism for it is missing.**
Location: ADR-49, "Review evidence carries over only where it proves something the move did not change … `sdlc.patch`"; "Why": "keeps freshness computed and exact"; SDLC Delivery paragraph; SPEC-18 spec-map row.
Problem:
- A test or code reviewer reads a read-only copy of the whole subject (Make and prove table), so its verdict depends on the base code around the patch. After a clean rebase the patch is identical but that context is not. The evidence then holds without its real inputs matching, which is what I-05 forbids, and calling it "exact" overstates it. The mitigation (the mechanical suite catches semantic conflicts) is reasonable, but it is a deliberate trade-off, not exactness.
- The gate (I-04, `evidence`) must accept evidence whose subject is a patch derived from the delivered commit. That needs a kernel-neutral "derived subject" relation: the factory maps a commit to its patch, and the freshness function and gate use the mapping. It is a kernel `evidence` and SDK change. The text assigns `sdlc.patch` only to SPEC-18, a factory specification that may not extend kernel rows.
Fix:
1. In ADR-49, name the carry-over as a bounded exception: "a review bound to a change subject is fresh for every subject whose change is identical; the base it read is recorded on the evidence and shown on the delivery card as 'carried over from <base>'." Either restrict such reviewers to the patch plus pinned items, or keep the project switch and default it as the operator chooses. Replace "exact" in the "Why".
2. Add the derived-subject mechanism to the record and to the SDK row (for example, `SubjectResolver.derive(subject, kind)`). Assign it to a kernel specification, either SPEC-28 or an extension of "Checks, evidence and freshness". SPEC-18 then only registers `sdlc.patch`.

**F5 (major). The queue's update step leaves the maker set and the place where conflict resolution runs undefined (I-15, I-09).**
Location: ADR-49, "the factory's workspace provider updates the subject onto it (for SDLC, a rebase in the private clone with hooks disabled)"; SDLC decisions table, `sdlc.merge_conflict`: "Rebase if conflicts are only in lockfiles or generated files".
Problem: a plain rebase is git plumbing and may run in the control plane. Resolving a lockfile or generated-file conflict means running the project's package manager or generator, which is work content, and I-09 says the control plane never executes it. Before v4.10 this rule had no executor. ADR-49 now makes Grindstone perform it inside the delivery path. Separately, nothing says who makes X′. If an agent resolves a conflict, that agent is a maker of X′ and may not prove its patch. If the factory regenerates a lockfile mechanically, the record should say whether that adds a maker.
Fix: add to ADR-49: "An updated subject inherits the makers of the subject it updates; whoever resolves a conflict joins them. Any update step that runs project tooling runs on a station (an agent station as a `work` task, or a check-station job whose output is a new subject), never in the control plane." State that a conflict outside the factory's mechanical rule becomes a work task, not only a decision.

**F6 (major). The continuation pack puts workspace knowledge in the kernel.**
Location: ADR-48, "a continuation pack the kernel assembles without a model call (the task, the workspace's diff against its base, …)"; Task concept, "Runs, sessions and turns".
Problem: "diff against its base" is a git concept, and workspaces are factory-owned through `WorkspaceProvider`. An RE workspace of blob sets has no "base" in that sense. If the kernel computes it, factory knowledge sits in the kernel (FF-12 after M2, I-18). If the kernel reads it off the agent station outside declared copy-out, that crosses B1 in a way the threat model does not list. M3-06 already has the SDLC factory carry "the previous run's diff" into a retry, so the factory is the right owner.
Fix: say that the workspace provider supplies the pack's change summary through an additive SDK operation (for example, `WorkspaceProvider.describe_changes(run)`), computed on its own terms and treated as agent output. Add it to the SDK row with SPEC-17 as its owner.

**F7 (major). A person who makes a subject can deadlock it in a single-operator instance.**
Location: ADR-50, "a person who made a subject cannot satisfy its `manual` check"; Task concept, "Person-made tasks"; S7.
Problem: the MVP has exactly one person. When the operator claims or takes over a task whose check plan includes a `manual` check, no prover can ever exist, so the task waits in verifying forever. Two smaller gaps: a claimed task is `running` with no run, so the liveness timeout does not apply, and nothing says how a claim is released.
Fix: add to ADR-50: "Claim and take-over are refused, with the reason shown, for a task whose required checks include one only a person can prove, unless the operator first replaces that check with a non-person method through a ChangeSet. A claim has no liveness timeout and is released by the operator, which returns the task to `queued`." Add a quality scenario under QG-1: a person-made subject never passes on evidence from its maker.

**F8 (major). ADR-52's nesting shape needs three clarifications before M4 and M5 specifications build on it.**
Location: ADR-52, "Nesting and mixing factories" and "Judging questions"; README, "Delegation modes".
Problem:
- "may consult specialists in the asking task's factory" limits the orchestrator to one factory per question. That contradicts mix and match: an SDLC implementer's question about a format RE documented needs an RE specialist.
- Two nesting mechanisms now coexist and their relation is unstated. `delegate` (agent-level, depth 2, I-17, used by S6 for exactly the RE → SDLC hand-off) and sub-goals (intent-level, "up to a depth cap"). A later specification cannot tell which one carries "an SDLC build spins off an RE goal that spins off a parser build".
- "Goals connect only through interface items" conflicts with "may contain sub-goals", since containment is itself a connection. Nor does the text say whether a sub-goal inside the chartered root's scope counts as `scope_expansion`, which under I-07 decides whether autonomous nesting can work at all (see F1).
Fix:
- Replace with "may consult specialists of any enabled factory, through ordinary matching".
- Add: "`delegate` remains the bounded in-task hand-off (I-17); a sub-goal is planned work created by an approved ChangeSet, with its own lifecycle, connected to its parent by containment and to sibling work by interface items; results cross either way only as pinned items and blobs (I-19)."
- List "whether a sub-goal within a chartered scope is scope expansion" among the questions the R2 addendum answers, or open it as a Q-nn now, because the hard requirement depends on it.

## Checked and found sound

- Counts: fifty-three records with index rows 48–53, twenty-eight specifications, S1–S7 and "Seven scenarios", the K/S ID table and the playbook template line all agree. FF-13 traceability reports 0 violations, and `uv run pytest -q` passes (43 tests).
- Status pointers: every record ADR-48 to ADR-53 names as amended carries a matching status line and index note (ADR-06, 12, 14, 16, 23, 27, 28, 29, 30, 36, 37, 38, 40, 41, 45). Beyond ADR-08 and ADR-31 (F10), no accepted record's decision text was edited.
- Module map and FF-01: no new module dependency is needed. The queue runs mechanical checks through `evidence`, risk classes live in `delivery`, accounts in `stations`, principals in `surface` and `decisions`, and person-made tasks in `work`. `store`'s lane gains only an opaque account key. The kernel imports no factory, and the orchestrator is a kernel capability whose playbooks arrive as factory data.
- I-08: take-over hands work over as a bundle through the authenticated API, and nothing unverified is pushed by Grindstone. `adopt` runs with hooks disabled (QS-06) and refuses non-descendants of the pinned base. A push never makes a maker (B3).
- I-15: makers are a set, the check plan rejects any prover among them, and "what different means" covers every maker and anyone who delegated to one. The orchestrator never makes, proves or reviews work it directed.
- I-05 for mechanical evidence: a rebase is a new subject, and the mechanical suite re-runs on the merge result. ADR-49 correctly rejects pretending the commit identity survives.
- ADR-51: agents never hold REST or operator tokens and act only through the per-task surface, which keeps B1 intact and ADR-16's reasoning. MVP behaviour is unchanged (operator and policy only), and the SPEC-04 and SPEC-10 forward notes are accurate.
- I-14: charters are added to the configuration agents never change, and only the operator issues them, so no self-delegation is possible.
- Scope: the MVP keeps classification and batching only. Policy or orchestrator delivery waits for R2, so the auto-merge non-goal holds. Grindstone merging after a person's `delivery` answer is correctly distinguished from auto-merge, and DEC-0904's forward note says so.
- ADR-48: one credential per account, given only to that account's sessions, matches I-09 and narrows the accepted residual risk. Q-12 is the right guard on provider terms. Native resume stays on its account.
- The forward notes in SPEC-02 and SPEC-04 to SPEC-10 are additive, name the owning M3 specification and keep every M1 decision (lanes, secrets, REQ-0613, DEC-0702, the port's `accept`, DEC-0904, REQ-1004) in force for M1 and M2.
- The mix-and-match requirement in shape: one orchestrator per project, goals over any enabled factories in any direction, factories that never nest, and interface items as the boundary. Nothing else in the tabs still assumes one factory per goal, apart from the consult restriction in F8.
